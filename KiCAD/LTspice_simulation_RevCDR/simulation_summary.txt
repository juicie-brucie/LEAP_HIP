1. Q1 SOLENOID DRIVE  (solenoid_drive.cir)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Circuit: IRLZ44N → 100 µH / 5 Ω solenoid, 1N4007 flyback, 12 V rail
Gate drive: Arduino 5 V logic → 220 Ω series → 10 kΩ pulldown

Results:
  Flyback spike (turn-off):    13.44 V  (+1.44 V above 12 V rail)
  Steady-state current:         2.389 A  (expected 12/5 = 2.400 A, 99.6%)
  Gate voltage at saturation:   4.89 V  (Vgs(th) ≈ 2 V — fully enhanced)
  L/R rise time constant:       20 µs   (100 µH / 5 Ω)
  Time to 99% steady-state:    ~100 µs  (5τ)
  MOSFET power dissipation:    ~0.12 W  (I² × Rds(on) ≈ 22 mΩ)
  Solenoid coil dissipation:   28.8 W   (I² × R = 2.4² × 5)

Assessment: PASS — no electrical concerns.
  The flyback spike is comfortably below the IRLZ44N's 55 V Vds rating.
  The 1N4007 clamps within one forward-voltage drop above the supply.
  MOSFET runs cool. The solenoid coil dissipates 28.8 W; confirm your
  specific solenoid's duty-cycle rating for the 5 s test run (= 144 J
  into the winding). 45 s worst-case = 1296 J.

Note: The MODPEX SPICE model uses Vgs(th) = 2.09 V, slightly above the
datasheet max of 2 V. Real parts typically switch at ~1.5 V, so actual
turn-on in hardware will be marginally faster than simulated.

Gate drive: same as Q1

Results:
  Flyback spike (turn-off):    12.80 V  (+0.80 V above 12 V rail)
  Steady-state coil current:   30.00 mA (expected 12/400 = 30.0 mA, exact)
  Gate voltage at saturation:   4.89 V
  L/R rise time constant:       1.1 ms  (440 mH / 400 Ω)
  Relay pick-up time:          ~5.5 ms  (5τ)
  Coil power dissipation:      360 mW   (30 mA × 12 V, continuous-rated)

Assessment: PASS — no electrical concerns.
  Spike is even smaller than the solenoid because the 400 Ω coil resistance
  dissipates stored energy faster. D2 barely conducts. Relay picks up well
  before the igniter circuit becomes live. Coil is rated for continuous duty.

Note: Consider replacing Q2 with a 2N7002 (SOT-23, 60 V / 115 mA). The
IRLZ44N is heavily over-specified at 47 A / 55 V driving a 30 mA load.

3. BUTTON DEBOUNCE RC FILTER  (button_debounce.cir)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Circuit: Button → 10 Ω contact resistance → Arduino pin
         10 kΩ pulldown + 100 nF filter cap to GND
Bounce model: 4 × 0.5 ms contact opens after initial press, settles at 5 ms

ATmega328P Schmitt trigger thresholds (5 V supply):
  HIGH threshold (Vih): 3.5 V  (0.7 × Vcc)
  LOW  threshold (Vil): 1.5 V  (0.3 × Vcc)

Results (100 nF, current Rev B):
  RC time constant:             1.0 ms
  Voltage hold above Vih during bounce: 0.36 ms
  Pin voltage at end of 0.5 ms bounce: ~0.0 V  (full dropout)
  Glitch edges seen by Arduino: 4  (false LOW→HIGH transitions)

Assessment: FAIL for hardware debounce alone with 100 nF.
  Tactile switches typically bounce for 1–20 ms. Every 0.5 ms bounce in
  the model drops the pin fully to 0 V and back — the Arduino would
  interpret each as a distinct press event.

Recommended fix:
  Hardware: Increase Cfilt from 100 nF to 2.2 µF (τ = 22 ms).
            A 0.5 ms bounce then drops pin by only 2.2% of supply (<0.11 V).
            Cap should be ceramic or film (avoid electrolytics at this value
            if PCB space allows; a 2.2 µF X5R 0805 is fine).

  Firmware: Regardless of cap value, implement software debounce on the
            ARM and FIRE buttons:
              1. Detect rising edge
              2. Wait 20 ms
              3. Re-read pin — accept only if still HIGH
            For the FIRE button, require ARM to be held first (already
            in planned two-action firmware sequence).

  Both measures together are strongly recommended for a safety-critical
  fire circuit. Hardware filter removes glitches before the ISR or
  polling loop; firmware debounce provides a second independent layer.
